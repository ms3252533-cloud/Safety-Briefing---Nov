Cold Stress & Body Temperature Management — Early Morning Winter Shifts at RUH
⚠ Cold Stress & Thermal Risk
What It Means
How It Appears
Why Dangerous
Correct Practice
Safety Message
What It Means

Riyadh winter temperatures regularly drop to 5–10°C in the early morning hours, with windchill on open ramp areas reducing the felt temperature further. Cold stress occurs when the body loses heat faster than it can produce it. For GSE technicians starting shifts before sunrise — handling metal components, working outdoors or in unheated bays, and remaining in one position for extended periods — cold stress is a real and underestimated occupational hazard that directly impairs the ability to work safely.

How It Appears in a GSE Maintenance Workshop
  • A technician arriving for a 05:00 shift works outdoors on ramp equipment in a light uniform — hands become numb within 20 minutes, reducing grip strength and fine motor control needed for fastener work
  • Prolonged stationary tasks in an unheated bay — inspection, documentation, or waiting for a part — allow body temperature to drop without the warming effect of physical activity
  • A technician who does not report feeling cold because they consider it normal or unimportant continues working with impaired dexterity and reduced concentration through a safety-critical task
  • Wet conditions combined with cold — dew, condensation on metal surfaces, or early morning rain — accelerate heat loss significantly and increase the risk of cold injury to exposed hands and face
Why It Is Dangerous
  • Cold reduces fine motor dexterity measurably — hands operating at reduced temperature lose the precision needed for torque work, electrical connector fitting, and hydraulic line coupling, increasing the probability of maintenance errors
  • Cognitive function slows under cold stress — decision-making, attention, and task sequencing are all impaired before the technician experiences any discomfort severe enough to report or recognise
  • Shivering, which begins as an early cold stress response, reduces stability and hand steadiness — a technician who is shivering cannot hold a tool or a component with consistent control
  • Cold stress symptoms — numbness, pale skin, confusion, excessive shivering — can progress to hypothermia if exposure is prolonged and unaddressed; at that stage the person cannot accurately assess their own condition
Correct Practice
  • Wear appropriate cold-weather layers for early morning shifts from November to February — a base layer, insulating mid-layer, and wind-resistant outer layer are the minimum for pre-dawn outdoor work at RUH
  • Keep hands covered with appropriate work gloves rated for cold conditions and change to dry gloves if hands become wet — cold and wet together accelerate heat loss and numb hands far faster than cold alone
  • Take regular warm breaks during extended cold exposure — a 10-minute warm break every 60–90 minutes of outdoor cold-environment work restores dexterity and concentration to safe working levels
  • Report numbness, persistent shivering, or difficulty concentrating during cold conditions to your supervisor immediately — these are physiological warning signs, not personal complaints, and must be acted on
Safety Message
🧊 "Cold is not uncomfortable — cold is a performance impairment. Numb hands make errors on fasteners. A cold mind misses steps. Dress for the temperature before the shift starts, not after you are already cold."
